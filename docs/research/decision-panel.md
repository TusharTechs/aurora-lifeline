## Judge: Senior Google Cloud / Google DeepMind engineer. Heaviest weight on whether Google AI is essential rather than decorative, and on whether the prototype actually runs end-to-end on a live link. I know Google's own weather and geospatial AI (WeatherNext, Weather Lab, Earth Engine, AlphaEarth, Flood Hub), and chatbot wrappers do not impress me.

**Winner:** C1 AURORA-Lifeline (Track 05), cut down to a thin, validated vertical slice for the 30 Sep submission and deepened for Demo Day  
**Runner-up:** C3 PlumeTrace + Airshed Grid (Track 02)

**Scores and rationale**

- **C1** fit 5, AI/tech 4, reach 4, deploy 4, impact 4, saturation 3, feasibility 3, data 4.5. It covers every clause of the T05 challenge text: surge simulation, rainfall damage pathways, exposure of power grids, arterial roads and medical shelters, and automated advisories to municipal and DM authorities. Parametric insurance, which the track problem statement names, is covered too. Google AI sits on the critical path, not on the side. Each of the 50 WeatherNext Cyclones ensemble members is carried through to consequence probabilities, which is the correct way to use an AI ensemble. Gemini multimodal parses IMD bulletins (issued as PDFs and track graphics) and checks field photos and video that update asset states. The CAP drafting in many languages is the commodity part. The real differentiator is non-Google OR/graph work: time-dependent reachability ('cut off at T-14h') and action optimisation. That is fine, but Gemini has to be shown driving it, not just narrating it. I checked the strongest competitor's public ShadowCast GitHub README (Apache-2.0, live on Vercel plus Cloud Run). It lists 'no arterial routing beyond identifying cut roads' as a limitation and publishes an Amphan AUC of 0.44 with a single-storm outage calibration. So the access-loss-timing whitespace is real, but the team must clearly beat that bar. I also checked the Weather Lab guide (developers.google.com/weathernext/guides/weatherlab). Ensemble tracks in CSV/ATCF exist only from about 2023 onward. Real-time data is under experimental terms and historical data is CC BY 4.0. So Fani and Amphan backtests cannot use WeatherNext and must use IBTrACS or ECMWF. Michaung, Dana, Fengal, Montha and BOB 06 can. The Earth Engine docs (noncommercial tiers; transition to commercial) confirm monthly EECU quotas since Apr 2026 and a paid requirement for government operational use. That is fine if EE runs only as a batch precompute job. The scope as listed is a 3+ month build, and the shortlist is judged on what is submitted by 30 Sep. Feasibility is the main discount.
- **C2** fit 4.5, AI/tech 3.5, reach 5, deploy 3, impact 4, saturation 2, feasibility 4, data 2.5. It fits the T01 text closely. The Viksit Gram Panchayat Plan / Gati Shakti export hook under VB-G RAM G is clever and timely, and it reaches all ~2.6 lakh GPs. Gemini does real work (audio to structured requests, cross-lingual clustering with gemini-embedding-2), but it is the same intake-classify-rank pattern that won edition 1 (Praja Svaram). Samadhan Didi now does it nationally via BHASHINI, and ~50 public T1 repos do it too, several already with LGD resolution and BigQuery joins. The recommendation engine is deterministic joins, and the Earth Engine before/after view is decorative. No real citizen-demand data exists, so the demo core is synthetic. eGramSwaraj is CAPTCHA-gated, and SabhaSaar and Meri Panchayat are not open feeds. A MoPR pilot therefore depends on integrations that cannot happen in weeks.
- **C3** fit 4.5, AI/tech 4, reach 4, deploy 3, impact 4, saturation 3.5, feasibility 3.5, data 4. It makes the most interesting use of Google-native AI among the non-T05 options. AlphaEarth satellite-embedding look-alike search for unregistered kilns and dumps is genuinely meaningful, and Gemini classifying the source in a citizen photo is essential multimodal work. The physics is weaker than the pitch. Sentinel-5P at about 5 km cannot corroborate a single plume. WeatherNext winds at 0.25 degrees are coarse for urban back-trajectories. Polar-orbit FIRMS misses the post-3 pm fires unless geostationary products are added. The TimesFM forecasting rebuilds what the Google Air Quality API already offers (500 m, 96 h, CPCB index); a Google judge will ask why it does not simply consume that API. The Sameer app already routes photo complaints in NCR, and SPCB adoption is slow, so deployability is moderate. The data is real and public, but citizen photos have to be seeded for the demo.
- **C4** fit 4.5, AI/tech 3.5, reach 4.5, deploy 2.5, impact 4, saturation 2.5, feasibility 4, data 2. It maps squarely onto the T03 text, and the pain is well documented (average PHC stocks 90 of 171 EDL medicines). Gemini reading stock-register photos, with a Gemma offline fallback, is meaningful. But no public facility-level stock data exists. AI.FORECAST on synthetic DVDMS-shaped series only shows the plumbing, not skill. That is exactly the 'unverifiable metrics' failure mode from edition 1. DVDMS and e-Aushadhi are login-only, so any pilot needs a state NHM data-sharing agreement, which does not happen in weeks. About 25 lookalike repos exist, one already using TimesFM.
- **C5** fit 3.5, AI/tech 3.5, reach 4.5, deploy 3, impact 4, saturation 1.5, feasibility 4, data 4. Gemini turning ICAR-CRIDA district contingency-plan PDFs into executable rules, triggered by WeatherNext ensemble probabilities, is a real and non-decorative use of AI. The pest-nowcast aggregation is a nice touch. But the track asks for regenerative crop recommendations and disease diagnostics, which the playbook covers only partly. T04 is also the most saturated space, both on GitHub (~27 repos) and in the state/Google stack (Bharat-VISTAAR, Kisan e-Mitra, Krishi-DSS, Google ALU/AMED, and a 2025 AI monsoon-onset SMS pilot that reached 3.9 crore farmers). It is hard to argue why a ministry needs this over what it already runs.
- **C6** fit 4, AI/tech 3.5, reach 3.5, deploy 4, impact 3.5, saturation 3.5, feasibility 4, data 3.5. It is a feasible, focused subset of C1, and no other T05 repo takes a health angle. But it answers only the medical-shelter part of a challenge that also asks for power grids and arterial roads in general, and it covers fewer people. Patient-level data (dialysis patients, expected deliveries) is not public. HMIS gives only aggregates and PMNDP gives centre lists, and anything finer raises PII concerns. So the 'which patients are affected' layer would be synthetic or coarse. Google AI use is the same as C1 at smaller scale. It works better as a demo lens inside C1 than as a standalone entry.

**Decisive reasons**

- Highest weighted score on the official criteria (about 4.2 of 5, against about 4.0 for C3 and C2). It covers every clause of the T05 challenge text: surge, rain damage pathways, grid/road/shelter exposure, advisory dispatch and parametric liquidity.
- Google AI is essential, not decorative. It carries each WeatherNext Cyclones / Weather Lab 50-member ensemble through to consequence probabilities, which is the correct use of an AI ensemble and the story Google DeepMind itself tells. Gemini multimodal handles IMD PDF bulletins and track graphics, and verifies field photos and video that change asset states.
- The whitespace is verified. The brief found no public Indian system that computes pre-landfall hospital/shelter access loss or population without power. The strongest competitor's README (ShadowCast) lists 'no arterial routing beyond identifying cut roads' as a limitation, so time-dependent reachability ('cut off at T-14h') plus action optimisation is a real differentiator.
- It runs on real open data end-to-end (IBTrACS, ECMWF open data, Weather Lab historical CC BY 4.0, OSM, Open Buildings, WorldPop, IMERG, Sentinel-1, Copernicus DEM, Sachet CAP). Backtests on real events give verifiable metrics, avoiding edition 1's 'unverifiable metrics' and 'synthetic crutch' failure modes that C2 and C4 fall into.
- It can be piloted within weeks. CAP 1.2 output matches NDMA Sachet, SDMAs and district collectors are the natural users, and precomputed results can be served from a CDN. The Oct-Dec cyclone season falls right after Demo Day, and India hosted the BRICS DRR ministerial in Jul 2026, which fits the Resilience/Cooperation framing.
- T05 has far less credible competition (~30 repos, one strong) than T1 (~50), T3 (~25) or T4 (~27 plus a crowded government stack).

**Modifications to the winner**

- Cut scope hard for the 30 Sep submission, because the Top-20 shortlist is judged on that build. Ship one fully working replay of Cyclone Montha (Oct 2025, Andhra Pradesh) with its forecasts replayed as issued. The chain: IMD bulletin PDF, Gemini 3.7 Flash structured extraction, WeatherNext Cyclones 50 members plus ECMWF ENS, per-member Holland wind with over-land decay, HAND-based rain flooding, and connected-bathtub surge scaled to INCOIS guidance where available. - Make the network cascade the hero and name it against the incumbent. Show a time slider where facilities turn grey as their last road link floods ('Srikakulam CHC isolated at T-14h in 38 of 50 members'). - Graft in Google OR-Tools (open source) for the action optimiser: a small MILP or assignment model for pre-positioning earthmovers, DG sets and medical kits, and for village-to-shelter assignment under capacity and road-closure-time limits. - Make Gemini's role clearly load-bearing, not narrative. (a) Build an ADK or function-calling agent over the precomputed BigQuery tables, so officers can ask 'which PHCs lose access before landfall in more than half of the members?' and get grounded, cited answers in structured output. (b) Add a multimodal field-report verifier that checks location/time consistency, estimates water depth and whether a road is passable from photos or video, and feeds a Bayesian update of edge states that recomputes the cascade. Show one report reopening a road live.
- Publish honest skill scores the way ShadowCast does, and extend them to access. Validate road/bridge closure predictions against public SDMA situation reports (Gemini can extract closures from sitrep PDFs, a second essential Gemini job). Validate flood extent against Sentinel-1 via Earth Engine, and power loss against NASA Black Marble VIIRS night-light loss. Report out-of-sample results and failures. WeatherNext data exists only from about 2023, so pre-2023 storms (Fani, Amphan) must use IBTrACS/ECMWF, and the slides should say so.
- Add the inland rain pathway using BOB 06 (Sep 2026), where 56 of about 74 deaths were in UP. Integrate the Google Flood Hub API (riverine and flash-flood forecasts) for inland districts. This turns 'coastal only' into national depth and adds another meaningful Google AI integration that ShadowCast lacks.
- Graft C6's health lens as a view mode, not a separate product. Show health facilities with their access and power timelines, public PMNDP dialysis-centre locations, and HMIS block-level expected-delivery aggregates for kit sizing. Use no patient-level or personal data.
- Engineer for a live demo that cannot fail. Run all Earth Engine work as batch precompute (noncommercial tier for the prototype, with a documented commercial-licence path for operational use). Serve PMTiles/GeoJSON from Cloud Storage plus Cloud CDN, with Cloud Run for the agent. Call Gemini through the paid Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform/Vertex endpoint, not AI Studio Tier-1 (the $10-per-10-minutes cap is a demo trap). Cache the demo storm's outputs and keep a load-test screenshot for the '10k concurrent users' claim.
- Get licensing and CAP compliance right. Validate CAP output against the CAP 1.2 XSD and NDMA's profile, and show dispatch to a mock endpoint rather than claiming a Sachet integration. Note that real-time Weather Lab data is under experimental terms and historical data is CC BY 4.0. Use OSM/Open Buildings shelters unless OSDMA grants permission for its 'All Rights Reserved' list. Cite all reused OSS.
- Show the BRICS/APAC transfer with one non-India replay that needs no new code: Cyclone Remal (May 2024, Bangladesh and West Bengal) or Mocha (2023, Myanmar/Bangladesh), both within the WeatherNext period. Keep parametric insurance to one district trigger-probability panel, and add a CDRI/insurer-facing line on the slide rather than building a finance product.

**Biggest risk:** Scope and end-to-end reliability. The pipeline as described is a quarter's work, and the shortlist is decided on what is live by 30 Sep. An over-engineered stack that half-works, or a map plus CAP draft that looks like a thinner ShadowCast, will not survive about 2,000 submissions. A related risk: if the hazard physics (connected-bathtub surge, Holland without land decay) is not visibly validated, domain reviewers (IMD/INCOIS/NDMA) will discount everything downstream. If the non-Google cascade and optimiser carry all the value while Gemini only writes advisories, a Google judge will call the AI decorative.

**What would change my mind:** I would drop C1 in any of four cases. First, if by 30 Sep the team cannot show a working, backtested access-loss timeline on one real storm (only hazard maps plus CAP drafts). Second, if another public T05 entry (including a ShadowCast update) ships time-dependent facility access loss with published validation first. Third, if the organisers name MoPR or MoHFW rather than NDMA/MHA as the pilot ministry. Fourth, if C3's team could show AlphaEarth look-alike search finding kilns confirmed against a public kiln inventory with measured precision, plus a Gemini photo source classifier with a reported confusion matrix. In that last case C3's more Google-native AI story and lower saturation would edge ahead. Separately, if Weather Lab's experimental real-time terms or Earth Engine's government-use licensing were confirmed to block even a free SDMA pilot, C1's deployability score would fall to about 3 and C2 or C3 would become competitive.

**Differentiators needed**

- Probabilistic time-resolved reachability: for every hospital, PHC, shelter and settlement cluster, the probability of losing road access and a latest-safe-departure window (P10-P90) across ensemble members. No competitor gives probability plus time on a graph (PRAVAAH is deterministic; ShadowCast has no routing).

- Validated flooded-road skill: predicted flooded or cut OSM segments scored against Sentinel-1 observed flooding for Montha, Michaung or Dana (CSI/recall). This is the first real ground truth for access loss in the field.
- Breadth ShadowCast lacks: at least 5 states, including Tamil Nadu (Michaung, Fengal), south Andhra (Montha) and Gujarat (Biparjoy, Arabian Sea), plus a one-click 'new storm anywhere on India's coast' run that finishes in under 10 minutes on a Cloud Run Job. - Gemini doing essential multimodal work beyond drafting, all on the demo path: (1) parse IMD bulletin PDFs and track graphics into structured forecast constraints that drive the model; (2) verify a field photo/voice/video report and flip an asset or edge state so the cascade recomputes live; (3) back-translation check of regional advisories. Use gemini-3.7-flash as the track names it.
- An actionable pre-positioning output: a greedy, explainable recommendation ('stage 3 earthmovers at X, which reaches 11 at-risk PHCs before their roads close; DG sets to these 6 hospitals losing grid power with no road access after T-6h'), with every number traced to the model and none invented by Gemini.
- Honesty that matches or exceeds ShadowCast: a validation page with per-storm skill, the misses, and labels for synthetic versus real data. Also a licensing page covering the EE commercial pilot path, WeatherNext experimental terms, OSDMA permission status, and ODbL share-alike for OSM-derived outputs.
- Deployability evidence for a ministry: a one-page SDMA/DDMA pilot SOP, a state onboarding recipe (OSM and Open Buildings plus state shelter list, in hours), a cost per state per season, and a Cloud Run + CDN architecture shown serving 10k concurrent users.
